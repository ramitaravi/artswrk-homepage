How we talk

Built for artists by artists. Our mission: shatter the starving artist stigma. Warm, direct, no jargon. We say what something costs and what it gets you. Short sentences. We never hide fees, and we never take a cut of an artist's pay.

  • Say "no commission" or "zero commission" — never "low fees."
  • Say "annual membership" or "annual unlock" — PRO and Basic are yearly, not monthly.
  • Artists are "artists." Hirers are "clients." Jobs PRO are "PRO jobs," not "premium."
Money line we reuse: "One booking pays the year off." (Avg booking: $250 on Basic, $500+ on PRO.)

How other marketplaces present pricing — patterns worth stealing

Lead with freeLessons.com opens with "It's free. For real. No subscriptions. No hidden fees," and DanceTeacherFinder puts its free teacher plan first. Our version: say "Free to post" and "free artist profile" louder — it's the strongest first line we have.
Keep each tier to 3–7 bulletsHigh-converting pricing pages use short scannable lists, never 20 features per card.
Make the recommended tier visually dominantA "Most popular" ribbon or tinted card. We already tag PRO "Most popular" and Premium "Best value" — keep those labels identical everywhere they appear.
Anchor the price against one bookingDanceTeacherFinder uses strikethrough pricing ($34.99 → $24.99). Ours is stronger: put "one booking pays the year off" directly next to the price.
Use pay-for-results languageIndeed: "Only pay when candidates click or apply" and "start and stop anytime." Our client equivalent: "Post free. Pay only when you want the applicants."
Cards sell, a table answersLessons.com pairs simple plan cards with a full comparison table underneath — cards for emotion, table for detail. The matrices above are that table.
Mute EnterpriseGive it a quieter card and a contact-style CTA so the three main tiers carry the decision.
